How to choose a private period tracker: six questions, and what Cyca keeps on your phone

Period data is some of the most personal information on your phone: when you bled, when you had sex, whether you are trying to get pregnant, whether a period is late. Picking a tracker is therefore less about pretty calendars and more about where that data lives. Here are the six questions worth asking of any period app, followed by Cyca’s answers taken only from its App Store listing, including what is free and what the one-time Pro purchase adds.

Six questions to ask any period tracker

  1. Where is my data stored? On the device only, or on the company’s servers? If it is on a server, it can be requested, breached or sold; if it is only on your phone, those risks shrink to the phone itself.
  2. Do I need an account? An email address or login ties every entry to you. An app that works without one cannot build a profile of you by design.
  3. Does it work offline? An app that functions in airplane mode is not sending your entries anywhere to do its job.
  4. Is there third-party tracking or advertising? Ad networks inside health apps are a common way cycle data leaks. Look for an explicit “no tracking” statement.
  5. What is free and what is paid, and is it a subscription? You want to know before you have six months of data in it.
  6. Does it claim to be medical? An app that calls its predictions estimates and says it is not contraception is being honest about what a calendar can do.

On the legal side: in the US, whether a health app is covered by HIPAA depends on who operates it and how, and the FTC’s interactive tool for app developers lists the other laws that may apply, such as the FTC Act and the Health Breach Notification Rule. Do not assume a consumer app is covered by the same rules as your doctor’s office. The simplest protection is data that never leaves your phone.

Switching from another app

Because Cyca has no account and no cloud, there is nothing to sign up for and nothing to sync. The practical cost of switching is the history you leave behind. Two tips: write down your last three period start dates before you delete the old app, and log them into Cyca on day one so the calendar is not empty. Then delete the old app’s account if it had one, which removes your data from their servers in a way that simply uninstalling does not.

Whatever app you pick, the questions above are the ones that matter. A warm design is nice; data that stays on your phone is the part you will care about in a year.
